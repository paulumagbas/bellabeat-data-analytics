# Marketing recommendations - reviewed drafts

Status: learner-developed recommendations refined through mentoring; not a final
case study report. No proposed campaign or experiment has been executed.
Product focus: Leaf as described in the historical case-study brief. Current
product capabilities and availability must be checked before operational use.

## 1. Test messaging about movement across the week

Observed evidence: in the same 32 participants with records on all seven weekday
categories (936 participant-days), Saturday had the highest participant-equal
average steps (8303.21) and Sunday the lowest (6950.02). This group pattern does
not establish individual inconsistency or the causes of weekday differences.

Hypothesis: prospective customers may respond to messaging about understanding
movement patterns across their week. Proposed message: "See your movement
across the week. Explore your daily activity patterns with Bellabeat Leaf."

Action: randomly test that message against a general wellness message using
comparable audience rules, offer, placement, and measurement window. Specify the
primary outcome and sample-size plan before launch. Candidate primary outcome:
purchase conversion per assigned eligible user when reliably measurable;
secondary outcome: click-through rate. No effect size or outcome is claimed.

Potential business value: identify effective acquisition messaging through a
measured experiment. Fitbit records contain no ad response, purchasing evidence,
or information establishing the best marketing day.

## 2. Research activity goals and test preference-based messaging

Observed evidence: 33 participants' mean recorded daily steps ranged from
916.13 to 16040.03. Recorded step variation does not identify goals, fitness
levels, motivations, or preferences.

Hypothesis: customers may have different activity goals and preferences; messages
matched to self-reported interests may perform differently from general messages.

Action: survey/interview Bellabeat prospects or customers about their goals.
Within self-reported groups, randomly test preference-matched versus general
messaging, holding other campaign elements comparable. Do not infer preference
segments from step counts. Do not assume the product offers personalized targets.

Measurement: survey participation describes research reach, not campaign success
or representativeness. For the messaging test, use a predefined purchase-conversion
outcome when measurable, with message engagement secondary. Compare variants
within the same preference group rather than treating differences between groups
as causal effects of personalization.

Potential business value: learn which messages resonate with stated customer
interests. Volunteer survey responses can be selective; no sales improvement is
established by the existing data.

## 3. Investigate sleep-recording barriers and test feature education

Observed evidence: 24 participants supplied 410 recorded sleep days, with 1-31
days per participant. These are Fitbit export patterns, not Bellabeat usage
telemetry. Missing records do not establish disengagement, nonwear, or cause.

Hypothesis: some Leaf users may benefit from clearer education about sleep
tracking, if Bellabeat-specific research identifies understanding or usage barriers.

Action: first interview/survey users about barriers and confirm product behaviour
and logging definitions. If warranted, randomly test an instructional onboarding
tutorial against existing onboarding. This is customer education/lifecycle
marketing, not a claim that onboarding caused the source dataset's gaps.

Measurement: define a primary comprehension or task-success measure. Treat tutorial
completion, reported usability, and appropriately defined recording frequency over
the same follow-up window as supporting measures. Predefine recording eligibility
and usable-record rules; more records alone do not establish better accuracy,
sleep quality, or wellness. No improvement has yet been measured.

Potential business value: assess feature understanding and support informed use.
Other barriers and technical recording causes may require different interventions.
